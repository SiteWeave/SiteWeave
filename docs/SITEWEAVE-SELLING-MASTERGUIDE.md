# SiteWeave Selling Masterguide

A practical guide for talking about SiteWeave like a human being — not like a brochure.

Read this out loud. If a line sounds like a press release, rewrite it before you use it.

---

## How to use this guide

- **Before a call:** skim “What we’re actually selling” and “Cold call.”
- **After a call:** send the leave-behind and offer the pilot scorecard.
- **During a pilot:** use the scorecard — not a feature checklist — to decide keep or kill.
- **Always:** listen more than you talk. A “no” is information, not an insult.

---

## What we’re actually selling

SiteWeave is construction job operations software.

In plain English: it helps a team **plan the job in the office, run it on the site, and show the client where things stand** — without rebuilding the story from texts, camera rolls, and spreadsheets every Friday.

**Say this:**

> “We’re not another generic project app with a hard hat on the logo. We’re the place where the office schedule, the field’s day, and the client update finally live in the same job.”

**Do not say:**

> “An all-in-one synergistic platform that streamlines stakeholder alignment across the construction lifecycle.”

That’s not how people talk. That’s how they hang up.

### What we are not

Be proud of the edges. Saying them builds trust.

- We are not full estimating.
- We are not accounting or ERP.
- We are not BIM.
- We are not a six-month implementation project dressed up as software.

If that’s what they need at the center, wish them well and walk. Pushing a bad fit burns the next conversation — and the one after that.

## Who you’re talking to

| Person | What keeps them up | What they want to hear |
|--------|--------------------|------------------------|
| Owner / GC | Money and reputation leaking through chaos | “Fewer surprises. Cleaner handoffs. Clients who trust the update.” |
| Project manager | Being the human router for every status question | “One plan. Less detective work. A report that doesn’t take your Friday night.” |
| Superintendent | Tools built for people who sit at desks | “Your phone. Your day. Issues and punch that don’t die in a camera roll.” |
| Smaller remodeler | Looking professional without enterprise theater | “Start a job from a template. Send a branded update. Look bigger than your headcount.” |

Ask who they are. Then sound like you asked.

## Product truth (so you don’t oversell)

Only sell what the product actually does.

### Office (web and desktop)

- Projects with phases, progress, and a Gantt schedule
- Start from scratch, from a template, or by importing MS Project XML
- Pull the schedule forward when you finish early (and keep client-facing dates steady if you want)
- Log weather impacts that can shift work downstream
- Smart emails before tasks start (advance notice — not only “you’re late”)
- Branded progress reports: one project or the whole org; client, internal, or brief; weekly / monthly / send now
- Org logo and colors on those reports
- Team roles and permissions
- Contacts for staff, clients, and trade partners — including people who don’t need a login
- Staff deployment (on a job, available, off, PTO)
- Calendar, messaging, activity history

### Field (especially mobile)

- My Day — what’s on you today
- Project updates and Site Day logs (completed work, weather, open issues — not a blank journal page)
- Field issues with severity, assignees, comments, and reminders
- Punch walkthrough: photo, location, note; after photos; punch PDF; client review link with sign-off
- Photos tied to the job, not lost in a camera roll
- Assign by team member, email, or phone — guest links when someone doesn’t have an account
- Pings by email or SMS (SMS only after real consent)

### Where it runs

Web, desktop, and mobile. Same organization. Same jobs. English and Spanish.

### Claims to avoid

- Don’t promise we replace Procore or Buildertrend. Ask whether their field and client update actually live there.
- Don’t invent change-order ERP or deep financials.
- Don’t pretend every crew will adopt anything. Adoption is the hard part — we designed for it (guest links, no-account assign, field-shaped capture), but we don’t control culture.

## How a great SiteWeave conversation feels

**Listen first.**  
Open with a real question and wait. The gold is in their words — “camera roll,” “Friday scramble,” “nobody told the plumber.” Use *their* language back to them.

**Empathy without theater.**  
You don’t need to perform sympathy. Just show you understand that status chasing is exhausting and that software fatigue is real.

**Curiosity.**  
Care how *this* company runs a job. A custom home builder and a multi-crew GC do not have the same Friday.

**Resilience.**  
Rejection is data. Wrong timing, wrong category, scared of “another tool.” Adjust. Stay warm for the next call.

**Integrity.**  
If SiteWeave isn’t the right fit, say so. That reputation outlasts any single deal.

**Value, not features.**  
They don’t buy a Gantt chart. They buy “we stopped arguing about whether we’re at 60% or 40%.”  
They don’t buy Site Day. They buy “I didn’t have to text the super three times to write the daily.”  
They don’t buy a progress report builder. They buy “the client got a clean update and I still went home.”

**Always leave with a next step.**  
A day, a job name, a person responsible. Never “let me know what you think.”

# Cold call

Speak this. Don’t recite it like a script tattooed on your arm. Pause. React. Sound like yourself.

**Time:** about a minute and a half if they engage. Shorter if they don’t.

---

### If a person answers

**You:**  
Hi [Name] — this is [Your name] with SiteWeave. I’ll keep this short. Have you got about a minute on how your team handles job status for clients?

*(If no: “Totally fair. Would tomorrow morning or afternoon be better for a quick call?” Book it. Stop selling.)*

**You:**  
When an owner or a client asks, “Where are we on the job?” — what’s the first thing you usually open? The schedule, a text thread, email, or a spreadsheet somebody updated this morning?

*(Shut up. Let them talk. Pick one phrase they used and nod it back.)*

**You:**  
Yeah — that gap is exactly why we built SiteWeave. We’re construction job ops. Office plan, field day, client update — same story. Not five apps and a Friday scavenger hunt.

I’m not calling to walk you through menus. I’m calling to see if one live job is worth a keep-or-kill pilot: your PM, one person in the field, one real client update. If Friday doesn’t get lighter, we leave. No hard feelings.

Does a job come to mind we could try next week — and who’s the PM I should include?

*(If they hesitate.)*  
Or I can send a one-page note and a simple pilot scorecard today. You look it over and either name a job or tell me it’s a no. Either answer is useful.

## SiteWeave — for people who don’t have time for a demo

### What this is

SiteWeave helps construction teams **plan the job in the office, run it on site, and show the client where things stand** — on web, desktop, and phone, in one organization.

### What this isn’t

Not estimating. Not accounting. Not BIM. Not a six-month “transformation.”

If that’s your center of gravity, we’re probably not the right tool — and we’d rather say that now.

### The Friday problem

Most teams don’t fail because people aren’t working hard. They fail because the story of the job is scattered:

- The schedule lives in one place  
- The truth from the field lives in texts and camera rolls  
- The client update gets rebuilt by hand every week  
- Somebody always says “nobody told me”

### What changes if SiteWeave is on the job

**In the office**  
Phases and schedule (including templates or an MS Project import). Advance notice before work starts. Weather and early finishes that can update the plan. Branded progress reports you can send on a cadence — or when someone asks.

**In the field**  
My Day. Site Day logs that pull from what actually happened. Issues when something’s wrong. Punch walks with a photo and a location. Reminders that reach people — including by text when they’ve agreed to get them.

**For the client**  
A clean, branded progress update. At closeout, a punch list they can review and sign off — without you hunting through phones.

### Who it’s for

GCs, project managers, supers, remodelers — anyone tired of being the glue between office, field, and client.

### A sensible next step

Skip the feature tour. Pick **one live job**. Put the schedule in. Have the PM and one field lead use it for a week or so. Send one real progress update. Then keep it or kill it using a simple scorecard — not a sales deck.

# Selling without a demo

Some managers will say, flat out: “I don’t watch software demos.” Believe them. They’re not being difficult — they’re protecting their calendar.

### What to say

> “Good. I’m not going to walk you through menus on a screen share. I’ll tell you what changes on a real job if SiteWeave is in the mix, what it doesn’t do, and how you’d prove it on one job without sitting through a tour. If it doesn’t map to your week in a few minutes, we stop.”

### Then ask

> “When a client asks where you are — what do you open first?”

Whatever they answer is your opening. Don’t fight it. Build from it.

### Paint a week (no UI)

**Monday** — The job is in SiteWeave. Template or MS Project. Crew on the project. Some people have logins; some are just contacts. People get notice before work starts instead of a last-minute “you’re up.”

**Midweek** — The super isn’t writing a novel. Site Day pulls from what got done, the weather, open issues. Problems become field issues with a real follow-up. Punch isn’t a pile of unmarked photos.

**Friday** — A branded update goes out. You’re not assembling it from five places. If you’re ahead internally, you can still keep the client-facing completion story steady.

**Closeout** — Walk the punch, export the list, send a review link, get a sign-off. Paper trail without a scavenger hunt.

### Three next steps they can pick

1. **Blind pilot** — Name a job. Start Monday. Decide keep or kill with the scorecard.  
2. **Working session (not a demo)** — Forty-five minutes. *Their* schedule goes in. You leave with a live job and a report ready — not a tour of Settings.  
3. **Outcome letter** — They name their two biggest pains. You reply in plain language: what SiteWeave helps with, and what it doesn’t. Then yes or no on a pilot.

Never end with “I’ll send some info and you can circle back.” End with a day and a name.

# Objection guide (say it out loud)

**“We already have Procore / Buildertrend / [big name].”**  
“Then the question isn’t whether you have software. It’s whether the field actually lives in it, and whether the client update writes itself or still takes a hero every Friday. If your stack already does plan, run, and prove end to end, keep it. We’re not here to pry you out of something that works.”

**“My crew won’t use another app.”**  
“That’s the real failure mode for most of this category — and you’re right to worry. We let you assign by email or phone without an account, share guest links, and capture the day like field work: photos, locations, issues, Site Day. I still won’t promise adoption. That’s why we pilot on one job with a scorecard instead of selling you a vision.”

**“We don’t have time to implement.”**  
“If implementation means a quarter and a binder, I wouldn’t buy us either. First job is template or import, invite your PM and super, turn on one weekly client report. We’re testing a job — not rolling out a religion.”

**“Just show me the product.”**  
“I can — but you’ll learn more from your own job in it for a couple of days than from me clicking around. Prefer a working session that ends with your project standing and a sample report queued. If you still want a tour after that, we’ll do it.”

**“What’s it cost?”**  
“Fair question. Price only stings when the value is fuzzy. What does a week of status chasing cost you now — in hours, and in one awkward client call? If a pilot doesn’t move that, don’t buy. I’ll help you kill it cleanly.”

**“Send me a deck.”**  
“I can send a one-pager and the pilot scorecard today. A slide deck of screenshots won’t tell you if Friday gets lighter. A one-job pilot will. Want me to include two suggested start dates?”

# Discovery questions (use sparingly)

You don’t need all of these. Pick two or three. Sound curious, not interrogative.

1. When a client asks where you are, what do you open first?  
2. Who builds the weekly status update — and about how long does it take?  
3. Where do punch items and site photos live today?  
4. How does a sub usually find out work is coming up?  
5. Do office and field usually tell the same story on percent complete?  
6. If you could only fix one thing this quarter — client updates, field capture, or handoffs — which one?  
7. Who has to say yes for a one-job pilot to be real?

Write down their words. Sell those words back later.

---

# Integrity checklist (before you push)

- [ ] Their pain is really plan / run / prove — not “we need ERP.”  
- [ ] There is a real job and a real person in the field for a pilot.  
- [ ] You’ve said out loud what SiteWeave is not.  
- [ ] Success is the scorecard — not how many features you named.  
- [ ] You are genuinely willing to recommend kill.

If you can’t check these, you’re not selling. You’re hoping.
